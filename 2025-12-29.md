# Time: The Tree of Life


At some point if my life this idea of time was introduced - and I've never questioned it.

Time is... These. Hours, minutes, days, weeks. The beginnings and endings, the cycles.

But why is an hour an hour and a minute a minute? 

How is it that this perception of reality modifies the way I interact with it?

Let's play around with that idea.

Time stops beating at the same cadence.

Time goes at 60 seconds a second - and it takes 60 seconds to live one of the old fashioned seconds. I'd be doing the same thing, and the difference would be as null as saying that a second takes a second to pass. Or that a meter are 1.09 yards. It doesn't mean anything.

Nature doesn't have a description other than the boundaries that are perceptive to the eye.

But even these boundaries can blur under the right conditions. 

Let's see, wouldn't that tell me more about a second than a second really does?

I see the tree, and I see the leaves in the tree. I can clearly denote that there is a point where the leave ends and the air begins - if it were. So at every instance where the leave, or the branch for that matter, stops being *it* and starts being *not it* that's where the tree ends, and the air starts.

And yes I understand that if I zoom in enough the difference would not be as clear, but from where I stand, it does look like there's a definite boundary, explicit, functional. Like my dermis. It exists, and it is right there, and I can see it. I can't see a second, so what is a second?

Well the fact that I can't see a second doesn't mean it does not exist. It is a fact that, if I wait long enough, the sun will dissapear from the sky - and I won't see it again until I wait a while longer.

That time I wait... That's something, isn't it?

Well... I am thinking of time as that. As something to wait for. As a series of instants interconnected across a string of space. Is that what time is?

Ok, let's say that I stop time right here right now. The leave stops swinging due to the air, and the birds are stopped in motion, and all the cells in their bodies as well. The rays of light that were about to hit the leave stop right there, just like the ones that just reached my eyes.

There comes a magician that can work at the speed of light, and instantly evaporates the leave.

The leave effectively stopped existing. I don't get to know that because that ray of light hasn't reached my eyes. So in one instant the leave exists, and at the other it stops existing. Have I broken existence? Probably, because it would take time by definition for this entity to do the act of evaporation. I think it would break physics to just disappear mass with no consequence.

So technically this magician can't exist. This magician is effectively impossible. If I were to see the leave dissapear, I would need to allow this time thing to pass, to allow the events to unfold in such a way that the energy that's expressed in the leave in such a way that it exists, stops expressing - just then I would not see it.

So it seems I need this time thing to even experience. Seems like without it, the world wouldn't have motion. I wouldn't have motion either, so I wouldn't exist either. Whatever this time is seems important.

How is it that it isn't the same thing as space, then? Why is it that I don't perceive it as just another dimension of space where objects, entities, you name it, can translate?

Well, I can't go backwards can I? I can't just take the road back to Cordoba from here using the time dimension rather than the "north-eastern" dimension of the X coordinate. But I can go back to Cordoba by moving in that direction while simultaneously moving in the same coordinate I used to come here in the first place.

So there is this thing called past time, and I can't access it.

What else can't I access? Well I can't see inside my heart without dying. I can't access the infrastucture of my living red cells without killing them. I can't access the knowledge that gave a why to the existence of my body, I can only intend to describe how it works.

Time seems to be in the same realm of other nature things that we know exist, but we don't know *why* they exist.

What if why is the wrong question to ask? Well, that would kill curiosity all together, and for sure I know that nature embedded curiosity within me. If there's anything I can do is to accept what is so I can then reason through why. Curiosity exist, therefore it must have a reason... Hmm... Where am I going with that?

What if curiosity exist, but there is no reason for it to exist? Well, what if anything exists for no reason whatsoever? Clearly. This is right here, why does it *need* a reason. Why doesn't it just *is*? 

So deny curiosity? Like denying the summer. It exists, it's right there. It may not have a why but it exists, and I embody it, and so I am curiosying - I'm peopling.

Ok, so I'll continue taking that there's not a necessary *why* and that it might be the wrong question to ask in the first place. I can ask what time is, and you can give it to me, and I can even try and see time from other angles, but always as slices of the entire pizza – almost as non-satisfactory per unit when you're hungry.

I can argue why time is definitely not, can't I?

Time is definitely not the tree. The tree is the consequence of events, and time superposes that. Time is in essence more fundamental than the tree, and therefore it can't lie in the same category. Time is more alike gravity in a sense, a geometrical ruleset. Geometry it is, drawing lines or curves. Time is more like the boundary between the leave and the air. Without it the tree wouldn't be tree, and with it the tree can tree calmly and obviously.

I can argue the tree exists within time and can't exist without it. Such as I do. I exist within time and can't exist without it - such that I can exist within space, and not without it... or can't i? I can't.

So asking what life would be without time would be equal to life without space. Non existence - you're taking off a core module of the game experience,  so it dissapears.

I am left with some arbitrary "yeah, this is what it is" and "don't question it" that are highly unsatisfactory. I am sure so long as I follow that idea that I'm the universe discovering itself that asking this is meaningful in so far as I can get further. I *still* have the intuition that I need to keep questioning. There *has* to be a why.

So that event, the one where the magician intervene. There is something happening there – whereby the event wouldn't unfold without the magician changing the thermodynamical structure of that particular system. You could set your unit of time at the moment where change unfolds, so change is the thing that time is about, and time is the arbitrary ruleset we've set to imagine how to describe the universe.

Let's start from the ground knowing that by understanding how and what and why time is, I can have a better relationship with it, just like I am having a better relationship with nature by acknowledging that cities are also part of nature and that I am too a node in a network. That the boundaries within me and "it" are nothing more than a comfortable illusion.

Change seems like the relationship with the now of a particular it, and the now of another particular it. Remove the relaitonship and change does not happen, and without change, this time dimension makes not a lot of sense. Without that movement, then ... hmm

So my phone is not moving. And I can "see" its boundaries. My phone is 3 dimensional, which means I can see all of its dimensions. If I grab it like I just did, and look at it frontally, it only has 2 dimensions. The existence of its third dimension is entirely dependent on the fact that my brain is storing memories about my phone. Remove my hypothalamus and other memory "storing" structures of my brain, and I wouldn't be able to tell this with so much certainty unless I do move the phone radially.

So the fact that I can only see time in one way, the fact that I can *only* see a particular set of geometrical structures doesn't tell me anything about what the thing on itself is really. I have to use my imagination for this, otherwise it won't be obvious. Looking at it won't be sufficient.

I know that I am aware. That this awareness is what allows me to experience space and time. Without it, I wouldn't be aware that it exists, by definition it would *not* exist because only what I am aware of exists at any given moment.

I am not aware if Dalma exists right now, she could've been completely vaporized a few minutes ago. It's not unless I call her or message here, or look at Findmy that I can effectively have a better prior of her existence. 

Things seem to come into existence for me when I am aware of them, and then we triangulate. I see the leave, and I see its movement in space. The leave moves in space not only because a wind blew it, but also because I was aware that it moved.

Let's challenge that.

I may not be able to see the water running through the pipes of the water treatment plant a few hundred meters away from here. But that water is bound to enter my body in the next few hours. I can't see the water but it sure exists. Well, not it doesn't, because that hasn't happened yet. How am I so sure that the water that I am seeing in my mind's eye *is* the actual water that will enter me – and so, how do I know that because I am looking at the screen right now, and my periphery allows me to see the trees moving, that does not count as some sort of awareness that the trees are there.

I have no idea if things exist outside of my conscious now. But things seems to unfold as if they did. Somehow my relationship with the tree, which is what allows *me* to see time passing by is allowing me to realize the existence of time, and is allowing time to unfold.

I could question what time is further from here, but then it would be better to question what I am. I am a natural being, born out of nature. I am nature. So as long as *I* nature, experience change, then time will be – unless I am not aware of it at some instance of my awareness capable nodes.

So why is consciousness this way?

Well, consciousness can't exist without change - and without change time doesn't exist either – or can't consciousness exist without change? I haven't ever experienced that, which limits the boundaries of what I can do.

Say consciousness can experience different lengths of change, and it is enhanced by having memory – which means that without it you can still *be* conscious, but not aware of existence as I might perceive it, and your flow of time may happen in a different cadence entirely. It doesn't negate time itself.

So time might be more fundamental than consciousness in the sense that consciousness is immersed within it like a fish is immersed in the water - a fish that is immersed in the water won't understand the sky even if you explain it to them in plain fishnglish – a smart fish with good imagination might though.

So whatever time is is beyond what I can *consciously* understand, because consciousness seems to be embedded within time. So time is definitely right there, meaning change is definitely happening due to entropy. We know entropy flows forwards, and we know entropy *could* flow backwards. If I am a fish and I can't see and experience the sky is because I don't know how to, not because I can't.

I picture this as some elaborate fractal. Life is this odd but beautiful negentropic instance that happens, and disappears - and for me as a fish in the water if feels like a slash of experience.

But maybe experience is not enough to describe what really is, and moreover, to understand why it is - that truck that is making sound in the background, can very well be as meaningful as an ant colony moving through a leave back to the cave. It's only important because it serves the purpose of the species - and it doesn't matter that is sophisticated, it is natural.

So ok, it seems clearer to me that I can't understand change by seeing it from the perspective of my conscious relationship with it. Then I have to zoom out.

And I see a 4D block, and everything is connected in this 4th dimension, all change in reality looks like the side of my phone. A single tied experience - which when look from afar looks like loops, and fractals, and beautiful disarray. 

So if this is the case why beauty? Well beauty is only what I can see from my consciousness – but for this universal being that could see it all it just looks like a bunch of mass clamped together. Indistinguishable, a mush of stuff.

Sorry, that makes absolutely no sense. That I get to live this experience, and that when I zoom out it just becomes absolutely incoherent. That almost sounds like a computer binary code - and it may as well give more value to the idea that this is a simulation. Maybe it is that.

Anyhow - Why a mush? All meaning and the form of a bike, and the purpose of things, all a mush. Or maybe there's like a geometrical shape to it? Like galaxies have. Difficult to know.

I am a fish in the sea, so how on earth am I going to imagine that Jupiter has those wrinkles and colored clouds. It would just not make any sense.

So - then - why does lived experience become incoherent when examined too closely?

Well, a book becomes incoherent when looked at too closely (coherent in terms of physics, incoherent in terms of meaning), and so when looked perpendicularly at a distance. Perspective seems to play a big role here, and if anything a good perspective is all I'm trying to get to here.

Say I then, am another consciousness separate from mine but aware of mine's existence. and I can move back and forth through time, and see existence's unfolding from my odd but totally possible perspective. A perspective of somebody that lives in the 5th dimension and can comfortably look at the 4th.

In that case, meaning is still right there. I can see Alex getting in the bike, and going to the store to leave it. And I can see him getting married with Dal, and I can see their kids. But there is a world where Dal does not come back from the mountain, and well, Alex can't know that from his current perspective, but 5th dimensional Alex knows for sure... Does he? 

Well that would contradict physics. And this all gets really fucking odd. So is 5th dimension Alex, if he existed, collapsing all the wave functions and creating a single stream of reality? If that were the case then yes for sure existence would happen in one single way.

But it doesn't seem like 5th dimension Alex exist because of quantum mechanics. If anything is more plausible that he absolutely can't exist and does not exist.

So consciousness seems to happen in this dimension, and therefore the many worlds interpretation is a coherent way to think of reality and still make sense of it - and time also happens in a myriad of different parallel dimensions of reality.

We know observation by definition implies entanglement. We can't just get out of that so lightly. What I'm arguing is that by definition of what we know, then reality can't be conscious of itself in so far as it looks through the multidimensional 5th layer. We are the only way reality can understand itself, and it would make sense aesthetically that it does, because it fits the geometry of fractals.

It seems coherent to think of time as a tree branching itself and evolving into becoming a bigger tree. And we're in the woods, there are multiple trees, thousands, millions of trees in the woods. And I can sit and observe each tree, one bigger, one younger, different types of trees. And maybe that's what nature is trying to tell me right now by putting these two trees for me to question and observe. Life is exactly like what I am seeing right now, and time isn't nothing more than what allows these trees to become. 

The beauty of this forest of life is the same beauty that I can see right now by just looking out and perceiving the trees. And the meaning behind time is the same meaning behind seasons, and behind cycles. It's an aesthetic meaning, an architectural one. A design choice, a form, a figure, a boundary in space that fits right in.

Right now this small tree I'm seeing dwarf by the other two big trees to the side of it happens to be blossoming some seeds. Why do I find it beautiful? What is beauty here? Well, the fact that I can see the tree for what it is - that I can *feel* it, and for a second become entangled with its architecture. If I get on the shenanigans of why a branch went in a particular way or not, I lose all of it all together.

Nature seems to communicate in systems design - and the answer as to why time is, is simply that it isn't. Time is a branch. Life is a tree. I'm in one branch of this particular tree. Time just is nothing less than self-recurring fractals - and from this perspective I feel much more in line with what is and what is not.

Giving life – therefore – has got to be one of the most beautiful acts one can do as a conscious being. It literally implies planting a tree of experience, and allowing it to help grow the forest of consciousness. Just like planting trees is beautiful, because it allows beauty to unfold in the form of nature - it allows microcosms of life, fungi, and flowers, and birds, and bees to coexist. 

Why is only obvious if just shut up and listen to nature's symbols, which can only be understood in the language of design and beauty.
